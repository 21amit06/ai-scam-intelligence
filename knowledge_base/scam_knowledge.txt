JOB SCAMS

Legitimate employers generally do not require applicants to pay upfront fees for job applications, interviews, training, equipment, or registration.

Warning signs of a job scam include:
- Requests for registration or processing fees.
- Requests for money before receiving a job.
- Very high salary for little or no experience.
- Pressure to act immediately.
- Requests for bank details or sensitive personal information.
- Communication only through unofficial email addresses or messaging apps.
- Fake interview or selection messages without a normal hiring process.

If a job offer asks the applicant to send money before employment, it should be treated as highly suspicious.


BANKING AND KYC SCAMS

Banking and KYC scams often pretend to come from banks or financial institutions.

Common warning signs include:
- Requests for OTP, PIN, CVV, or passwords.
- Messages claiming an account will be blocked immediately.
- Requests to click an unknown link to update KYC.
- Requests to install an unknown application.
- Urgent requests to verify account information.
- Fake customer-support numbers or links.

Banks should not require customers to reveal confidential authentication information such as OTPs or PINs.


PRIZE AND LOTTERY SCAMS

Prize scams tell victims that they have won money, a lottery, gift, or reward.

Warning signs include:
- You are told you won a prize you never entered.
- Requests for taxes, processing fees, or delivery charges.
- Requests for bank details to receive the prize.
- Pressure to pay immediately.
- Unofficial links or contact information.


INVESTMENT SCAMS

Investment scams promise unusually high or guaranteed returns.

Warning signs include:
- Guaranteed profits.
- Extremely high returns with little risk.
- Pressure to invest immediately.
- Requests to transfer money to personal accounts.
- Fake investment platforms.
- Referral schemes that depend heavily on recruiting others.


PHISHING

Phishing messages attempt to trick users into clicking malicious links or providing sensitive information.

Common warning signs:
- Suspicious URLs.
- Misspelled or unusual domains.
- Urgent account verification requests.
- Requests for passwords or OTPs.
- Threats that an account will be suspended.
- Links that do not match the claimed organization.


IMPERSONATION SCAMS

Impersonation scams pretend to be trusted people or organizations.

Examples include:
- Bank representatives.
- Government officials.
- Police officers.
- Company employees.
- Friends or family members.

Warning signs:
- Unexpected requests for money.
- Requests to share OTPs or passwords.
- Emergency stories designed to create panic.
- Requests to transfer money to unfamiliar accounts.
- Pressure not to contact the real organization.
